Interactions and assumptions

This page says what ligint recognizes and with which criteria. The criteria are geometric: they are applied to the coordinates of the model, together with a few chemical assumptions that the next sections list. Every threshold can be changed (see the command-line reference).

What it recognizes (default thresholds)

Type Criterion
Hydrogen bond D···A 2.2–3.5 Å, compatible donor and acceptor; with known H: D–H···A ≥ 120° and H···A ≤ 2.7 Å, otherwise antecedent angle ≥ 90° (amines with three substituents: N–H axis within 50°)
Salt bridge opposite charges, atoms ≤ 4.0 Å; with an H-bond on the same pair a single row "also hydrogen bond"
Electrostatic repulsion like charges ≤ 4.5 Å
Hydrophobic contact apolar C–C at 3.3–4.0 Å (carbons not bonded to N/O/P)
pi-stacking centroids ≤ 5.5 Å; parallel (normals ≤ 30°, planes 3.0–4.5 Å apart, offset ≤ 3.5 Å) or T-shaped (≥ 60°, offset ≤ 2.0 Å)
Cation-pi cation–centroid ≤ 6.0 Å, within 30° of the ring axis
Halogen bond Cl/Br/I···N/O/S ≤ 3.5 Å, C–X···A angle ≥ 140° (fluorine is not considered)
Metal coordination O/N with a lone pair or S within the per-element limit (Mg 2.5; Zn 2.6; Ca 2.8; ...)
Water bridge water 2.2–3.5 Å from both ligand and protein, ligand–O–protein angle 75–140°, without direct contact
Steric clash distance < sum of the van der Waals radii − 0.5 Å
Possible covalent bond distance < 2.0 Å

Chemical assumptions

Crystallographic PDB files have no hydrogens, so the ligand chemistry is inferred from the geometry:

  • Physiological pH: carboxylates, phosphates and sulfonates (two terminal O on the same atom) are anionic; aliphatic amines with only sp3 substituents, and amidines and guanidines not in a ring, are cationic.
  • Amides: an N bonded to a carbonyl C is not an acceptor. A ring N with a lone pair is an acceptor.
  • Metals: O/N coordinated to a metal are considered deprotonated (not donors) unless --metal-donors; His and other N bound to a metal make no H-bonds.
  • Histidine: donor and acceptor, never charged.
  • Protein: DNA/RNA are partners (phosphates, bases, stacking); if the PDB contains the protein hydrogens they are used.
  • PDB and PDBx/mmCIF files are read; the format is recognized from the content (see docs/INPUT_FORMATS.md: columns used, chains of several characters, limits; gzip-compressed files must be decompressed first).
  • Only the first model is read. For residues with alternate locations (altloc) the first conformation encountered is kept, or the one given with --altloc X (GUI: Alternate conformation): a residue that does not have it keeps its first one. The program reports for how many residues the requested conformation was used and warns if the file does not have it. The choice applies to the whole structure, ligand and waters included; occupancies are not considered.
  • Multi-residue ligands (disaccharides, glycans, dipeptides) covalently linked are a single ligand (as in PLIP); --no-merge keeps them separate.

SDF and MOL2

--sdf / --mol2 provide bond orders, explicit hydrogens and formal charges. The PDB ligand is matched to the template by coordinates (tolerance 0.6 Å, same number of heavy atoms): the file must have the same coordinates as the PDB. With hydrogens in the template, donors/acceptors and D–H···A angles are real; without H, or if the matching fails, the geometric heuristics are used (the program says so). SDF V3000 is not supported.

Presets and options for the comparison with PLIP

  • --hb-long 4.1: H-bonds with D···A up to 4.1 Å only if the H (real or ideal) can point to the acceptor.
  • --salt-centroid 5.5: salt bridges between the centroids of the charged groups (one contact per group).
  • --plip-like = --hb-long 4.1 --salt-centroid 5.5 --hal-max 4.0; explicit options take precedence.

They are off by default: --hb-long was confirmed on data not used for the tuning, --salt-centroid has a small and uncertain effect.

Interaction type names

The type column of the CSV, the Type column of the tables and the library constants use these names: Hydrogen bond, Salt bridge, Electrostatic repulsion, Hydrophobic contact, Apolar contact (backbone), pi-stacking (parallel), pi-stacking (T-shaped), Cation-pi, Halogen bond, Metal coordination, Water bridge, Steric clash and Possible covalent bond. Steric clashes and electrostatic repulsions are unfavorable contacts, listed with - in the favorable column. A possible covalent bond (distance below 2.0 A) is only a flag that the contact is too close to be a non-covalent one (it is listed with +). None of the three enters the fingerprint.
