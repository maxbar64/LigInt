Validation and limits

How to read the results

ligint applies geometric criteria to the atomic coordinates of a model and lists the contacts that satisfy them. Keep in mind:

  • A listed interaction means that the geometry of the model fits the criterion, not that the interaction contributes to binding, and not that it exists in solution. A missing interaction can be a real absence, a limit of the model (low resolution, a poorly defined ligand: look at the B-factor note) or a consequence of an assumption about the chemistry.
  • Hydrogens are normally absent from crystal structures, so donor and acceptor roles and charges are inferred. When a ligand is chemically unusual, or when the protonation state matters, provide an SDF or MOL2 file (templates).
  • Water bridges depend on the waters that the structure contains, and many of them are not well defined.
  • Unfavorable contacts (clashes, repulsions) are reported to help you judge a pose or a model; they are not errors of the program.
  • The thresholds are conventions. Changing them changes the results; the PLIP-like preset is one such change, chosen to get closer to another widely used program.

How the results were checked

ligint has been checked in several ways, none of which is a validation against experiment:

  • Automatic tests on synthetic fragments with known geometry and known expected outcome, on real complexes whose interactions were checked by hand, and on malformed input.
  • Comparison with PLIP on 150 complexes, reported in detail in Comparison with PLIP. The agreement is moderate and the disagreements are mostly conventions of the two programs, not errors of one of them.
  • Equivalence tests between the formats: the same structure read from PDB and from mmCIF gives the same results.

Known limits and differences from PLIP

  • PLIP counts fluorine halogen bonds too; ligint does not.
  • PLIP treats histidine as positively charged (about a third of the missing salt bridges); ligint does not.
  • PLIP counts hydrophobic contacts of carbons of polar residues and of heme; ligint limits them to hydrophobic residues.
  • Water bridges: PLIP applies its own filters (a donor already in an H-bond cannot form a bridge, at most two bridges per donor water); ligint does not and reports many more bridges (precision ≈ 40%).
  • PLIP does not analyze nucleic acids as partners; ligint does (not validated against a reference).
  • Not covered: SDF V3000, NMR structures with several models, pH values other than physiological, covalent bonds with the protein (only flagged as "possible covalent bond"), real SDF/MOL2 files from docking (only the 1EFY SDF and a synthetic MOL2 were tried).

Test status

Verified: Linux, GCC 13 and Clang 18 with -Wall -Wextra -Werror, Qt 6.4 with the offscreen platform (smoke test and screenshots), build with ASan+UBSan, installation and use as a CMake package. Not verified: macOS and Windows, Qt 5 (the code avoids Qt6-only APIs, but it was not compiled). The workflows in .github/workflows/ have never been run: the commands are the ones tried here locally on Linux (including -Werror, clang and sanitizers), but the first run on GitHub may need adjustments. The GUI was tried in offscreen mode: interactive use with mouse and dialog boxes was not tried by hand.
